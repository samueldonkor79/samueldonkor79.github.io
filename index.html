Background

Practitioner.
Researcher. Builder.

In 2014, I deployed a Business and Property Rate Management System for the Kumasi Metropolitan Assembly — Ghana's second-largest city government. Revenue collection increased by 60%. The Mayor issued a formal commendation.

Over the next decade, I built and maintained nine production systems across Ghana's core public institutions: pension management, healthcare, banking, education, licensing, and city governance. These systems serve millions of Ghanaians every day.

I am now applying for a PhD in Computer Science to develop the research methodology to study, improve, and scale AI-driven decision support for civic systems in resource-constrained, low-data-literacy contexts — the exact environments my software has operated in for twelve years.

PhD Research Agenda

Where Practice
Meets Research.

01
AI Decision Support for Civic Systems
How can AI-driven decision-support systems be designed for public sector institutions in low-data-literacy, resource-constrained contexts — surfacing uncertainty in ways non-expert users can act on responsibly?
02
HCI for Non-Expert Users
What interaction frameworks work for civic workers — hospital clerks, bank officers, pension administrators — who must make consequential decisions using AI systems without formal data or technical training?
03
Federated Learning for Government Data
How can multiple disconnected government agencies — each holding sensitive data — benefit from shared AI intelligence without centralising that data, preserving privacy and institutional sovereignty?
